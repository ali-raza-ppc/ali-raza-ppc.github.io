POAS vs ROAS: Why Profit on Ad Spend Matters More

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 7 min read · Nov 2026

ROAS tells you how much revenue your ads brought in. POAS tells you how much profit they brought in. If your products all have the same margin, the two move together. If they don't, ROAS can push budget towards the products that make you the least money.

Quick summary

  • POAS = gross profit from ads ÷ ad spend. Above 1.0 means ads paid for themselves.
  • ROAS treats a $100 sale at 20% margin the same as a $100 sale at 70% margin. POAS doesn't.
  • Start by calculating POAS in reporting, then use it for product and budget decisions.
  • Advanced setups send profit as the conversion value so Meta and Google optimise for it.

ROAS vs POAS in one example

Product AProduct B
Ad spend$1,000$1,000
Revenue$4,000$3,000
ROAS4.03.0
Gross margin20%60%
Gross profit$800$1,800
POAS0.81.8

By ROAS, Product A wins. By POAS, Product A loses money and Product B is more than twice as profitable. Scaling the "best ROAS" product here would grow revenue and shrink profit.

How to calculate POAS

POAS = (revenue − cost of goods − variable costs) ÷ ad spend

Variable costs usually include shipping, payment processing, packaging and an allowance for returns. Keep the definition consistent so you can compare over time.

  • POAS below 1.0: ads cost more than the gross profit they produced.
  • POAS of 1.0: break-even on gross profit (before fixed costs).
  • POAS above 1.0: ads are contributing profit towards fixed costs and net profit.

When POAS matters most

  • Catalogs with very different margins across products or collections
  • Stores running heavy discounts or bundles
  • Brands selling third-party products alongside own-brand products
  • Shopping and catalog campaigns where the algorithm picks which products to show

How to start using POAS

Level 1: Reporting

Export product-level sales from Shopify, add product costs, and calculate POAS by campaign, product set or collection. A Google Sheet is enough to start.

Level 2: Product decisions

Use POAS to decide which products to push in catalog ads, which to exclude, and which deserve their own campaigns. Low-margin bestsellers might work as entry products, but shouldn't absorb most of the budget.

Level 3: Profit as the conversion value

The advanced option is to send gross profit instead of revenue as the purchase value to Meta and Google, usually via server-side tracking. The platforms then optimise towards profit. This needs reliable cost data and careful setup, so I only recommend it once tracking is solid. See setting up the Meta Conversions API on Shopify.

POAS, ROAS and MER together

These metrics answer different questions:

  • ROAS: how much revenue did this campaign drive (in-platform)?
  • POAS: how much profit did it drive?
  • MER: how efficient is total ad spend across the business? See MER explained.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving shipping and payment fees out of costs
  • Using last year's product costs
  • Switching to profit-based bidding before tracking is reliable
  • Forgetting that a first order can lead to repeat purchases

Frequently asked questions

What is POAS?

POAS stands for profit on ad spend. It is the gross profit generated by ads divided by ad spend. A POAS above 1 means the ads generated more gross profit than they cost.

How do you calculate POAS?

POAS equals gross profit from ad-attributed orders divided by ad spend. Gross profit is revenue minus product cost and other variable costs such as shipping and payment fees. For example, 2,000 in gross profit from 1,500 in spend is a POAS of 1.33.

Is POAS better than ROAS?

POAS is better for judging profitability, especially when products have different margins. ROAS is simpler and available by default in ad platforms. Many brands report both and use POAS for budget and product decisions.
