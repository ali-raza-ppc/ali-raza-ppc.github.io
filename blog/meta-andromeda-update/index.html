Meta Andromeda Update (Andromeda 2.0): What Changed and What Advertisers Should Do

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 9 min read · Nov 2026

"Andromeda" has become one of the most searched topics in Meta advertising, and the questions got louder after Meta announced the next generation of the system in October 2026. Many advertisers are calling it Andromeda 2.0. Some blame it for unstable results; others say it rewards accounts that were already doing the right things.

Here's what the Meta Andromeda update actually is, what changed, and how I'm adjusting client accounts because of it.

Quick summary

  • Andromeda is Meta's ad retrieval system: it picks the shortlist of ads that could be shown to each person.
  • The October 2026 update (Andromeda 2.0) scales it up massively and adds models that understand what your ads are about.
  • Your creative now does more of the targeting. Distinct concepts matter more than ad count.
  • Keep structure simple, targeting broad, tracking clean, and feed the account new concepts regularly.

What is Meta Andromeda?

When someone opens Facebook or Instagram, Meta has to choose which of millions of eligible ads to show. That happens in stages:

  1. Retrieval: narrow millions of ads down to a few thousand candidates for this person.
  2. Ranking: score those candidates on predicted actions and value.
  3. Auction: combine bids and predicted value to pick the winner.

Andromeda is the first stage. Meta introduced it in an engineering post in December 2024, describing a large jump in the complexity of its retrieval models and an improvement in ad quality on the segments it tested. The key point: if your ad isn't retrieved, it never gets the chance to be ranked or win an auction.

What changed in Andromeda 2.0 (October 2026)

On 28 October 2026, Meta announced a next-generation retrieval system. Based on Meta's engineering announcement and coverage of it, the main changes are:

AreaOriginal Andromeda (2024)Andromeda 2.0 (2026)
ScaleGrouped and skipped many ads to stay fastOver a billion ads evaluated per second, roughly 100× the 2024 capacity
Model designOne main model of the userMixture of experts: specialised models for different interest areas
Understanding adsMostly from performance signalsNew GenRecs model reads ad text and images to understand what the ad is about
Ranking partnerGEM modelLarger GEM model using more user history

Meta says advertisers don't need to switch anything on, and it hasn't published a new "Andromeda playbook". Meta has also reported conversion improvements from these systems, but those are platform-wide averages, not a promise for any single account.

What this means for advertisers

1. Creative is now your targeting

With models that understand what an ad says and shows, the ad itself tells Meta who it's for. A creative about joint pain for runners will find a different audience than one about gifting for new parents, even with identical broad targeting. That's why "creative diversity" is the phrase you'll see attached to Andromeda everywhere.

2. Concepts beat variations

If ten ads use the same face, the same hook and the same message with small tweaks, the system effectively sees one idea. Meaningfully different concepts give retrieval more ways to match your product with different people. Think in terms of:

  • Angle: problem-solution, social proof, comparison, founder story, offer
  • Persona: who the ad speaks to and who appears in it
  • Format: UGC video, static, carousel, demo, before/after
  • Moment: when and why someone would use the product

3. Fragmented structures fight the system

Many ad sets with narrow interests split data and limit what retrieval can do. Simple, consolidated structures with broad audiences let the models find buyers. See my Meta ads account structure guide for the setup I use.

4. Fatigue can hit faster

When a strong concept gets shown efficiently to everyone it fits, it can saturate sooner. A steady pipeline of new concepts matters more than ever. My creative fatigue guide covers the warning signs.

My Andromeda checklist for client accounts

  1. Audit concepts, not ads. Write one sentence describing each live ad. Group ads that say the same thing. Most accounts have far fewer real concepts than they think.
  2. Fill the gaps. Brief new concepts for angles, personas and formats you're missing.
  3. Make the idea obvious early. Put the core message in the first frame and first line so the system and the viewer both understand it.
  4. Consolidate. Fewer campaigns and ad sets, broad or Advantage+ audiences, unless you have a clear reason to split.
  5. Test concepts properly. Give each new concept enough budget and time before judging it. My creative testing framework shows how.
  6. Track spend by concept. If one concept takes most of the budget, you're exposed when it fatigues.
  7. Keep tracking clean. Pixel plus Conversions API with good event match quality. Better signals mean better learning.

What Andromeda doesn't change

A better delivery system can't fix a weak offer, a slow landing page or broken tracking. If results dropped after the update, check the basics before blaming the algorithm. My guide on why Meta ads ROAS drops walks through the usual causes.

Common mistakes after the Andromeda update

  • Uploading dozens of near-identical ads and calling it "creative diversity"
  • Rebuilding narrow interest stacks to "control" delivery
  • Judging new concepts after one or two days
  • Assuming Meta's average performance gains will show up in every account
  • Ignoring offer, landing page and tracking problems

Frequently asked questions

What is the Meta Andromeda update?

Andromeda is Meta's ad retrieval system, the first stage of ad delivery that picks a shortlist of ads that could be shown to each person. Meta introduced it in December 2024 and announced a next-generation version, often called Andromeda 2.0, on 28 October 2026.

What changed in Meta Andromeda 2.0?

According to Meta's engineering announcement, the new version can evaluate over a billion ads per second, about 100 times the 2024 system, uses a mixture-of-experts design with specialised models for different interest areas, and works alongside a new GenRecs model that understands ad content and a larger GEM ranking model.

Do I need to change anything in my campaigns for Andromeda?

Meta says there is nothing to switch on. But because retrieval now relies more on what your ads say and show, accounts with genuinely different creative concepts, simple campaign structures and broad targeting are best placed to benefit.

How many ads should I run per ad set after Andromeda?

Meta has not published a recommended number. Focus on the number of distinct concepts rather than ad count. Ten small variations of one idea are treated much like one ad, while four clearly different angles give the system more to work with.
