Why Facebook Ads Get Clicks but No Sales (and What to Fix)

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 8 min read · Nov 2026

Few things are more frustrating than an ad with a great click-through rate and zero purchases. The good news: clicks with no sales is a symptom, and the cause is usually one of a handful of things you can check in order.

Quick summary

  • Check tracking first. Sometimes sales are happening but aren't reported.
  • Compare link clicks with landing page views to spot bad traffic.
  • Follow the funnel: landing page → add to cart → checkout → purchase.
  • Fix the stage where people drop off, not the whole campaign.

Step 1: Make sure it's not a tracking problem

Compare Shopify orders with Meta-reported purchases for the same days. If Shopify shows orders from Facebook traffic that Meta doesn't, your pixel or Conversions API setup needs fixing. Use Events Manager's Test Events to check the Purchase event fires once per order with value and currency.

Step 2: Check traffic quality

MetricWhat it tells you
Link clicks vs landing page viewsA big gap means people click but leave before the page loads (slow site or accidental clicks)
Breakdown by placementSome placements can send lots of cheap, low-intent clicks
Time on page / bounce rateVery short sessions point to a mismatch or poor page
Optimisation eventOptimising for link clicks finds clickers, not buyers

If your campaign optimises for traffic or landing page views, switch to purchases (or add to cart if purchases are too few). Meta delivers what you ask for.

Step 3: Find where the funnel breaks

  • Lots of views, few add to carts: product page problem (price, photos, reviews, unclear benefits, sizing).
  • Add to carts, few checkouts: cart surprises such as shipping costs, delivery times or a required account.
  • Checkouts, few purchases: payment options, trust, errors at checkout, or price shock at the final step.

10 common fixes

  1. Match the landing page to the ad. If the ad shows one product or offer, land on that product with that offer visible.
  2. Speed up the page, especially on mobile. Compress images and remove unused apps.
  3. Show price, shipping and returns clearly before checkout.
  4. Add reviews and real photos near the add-to-cart button.
  5. Offer express payment options such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay and PayPal.
  6. Remove distractions like pop-ups that appear instantly.
  7. Make sure the offer is competitive against similar products.
  8. Exclude placements that clearly send junk clicks, based on data.
  9. Optimise for purchases, not clicks.
  10. Retarget add-to-cart and checkout visitors with a reason to finish. See my abandoned cart retargeting guide.

When the problem is the ad itself

Curiosity-bait or clickbait creative can earn a high CTR from people who were never going to buy. If your best-CTR ads have the worst conversion rate, rewrite the hook so it qualifies buyers: show the product, the price point or who it's for early.

Know your numbers

Finally, check whether the sales you do get are profitable. A break-even ROAS tells you how many clicks you can afford per sale before an ad loses money.

Frequently asked questions

Why am I getting clicks but no sales on Facebook ads?

The usual causes are broken purchase tracking, low-quality clicks from certain placements, a gap between the ad promise and the landing page, a slow or confusing product page, unexpected shipping costs or checkout friction, and an offer that isn't competitive.

How many clicks should it take to get a sale?

It depends on price, niche and traffic temperature. Calculate your landing page conversion rate from your own data and compare it with your store's average from other traffic sources. A big gap points to a traffic or message problem.

Should I turn off ads that get clicks but no sales?

Not immediately. First confirm purchases are tracked correctly and that the ad has had enough spend to judge. Then check landing page views, add to cart and checkout rates to find where people drop off.
