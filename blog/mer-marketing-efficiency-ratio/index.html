MER Explained: The Blended Metric E-commerce Brands Should Track

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 7 min read · Nov 2026

Meta says it drove $50,000 in sales. Google says $30,000. TikTok says $10,000. Shopify shows $70,000 in total. Every platform claims credit for some of the same orders. MER cuts through this by looking at the whole business.

Quick summary

  • MER = total revenue ÷ total ad spend. Also called blended ROAS.
  • It doesn't depend on attribution, so it's hard to fool.
  • Use MER to set overall budgets and in-platform ROAS to optimise campaigns.
  • Calculate a target MER from your margins and profit goals.

How to calculate MER

MER = total revenue ÷ total ad spend (same time period)

If your store did $70,000 in revenue last month and you spent $20,000 across Meta, Google and TikTok, MER is 3.5.

Decide on your definitions and stick with them:

  • Revenue: gross or net of discounts and returns? Net is usually more useful.
  • Spend: only media spend, or also agency fees and creative costs?
  • Some brands also track new customer MER (aMER): new customer revenue ÷ ad spend.

MER vs ROAS

ROASMER
RevenueAttributed by one platformAll store revenue
SpendOne campaign or platformAll ad spend
Affected by attributionYesNo

ROAS is still useful. It tells you which ads and campaigns perform better. MER tells you whether the whole system is working.

How to set a target MER

Start with your contribution margin (revenue minus product cost, shipping, fees and returns). Then decide how much of that margin you're willing to spend on ads.

Example: contribution margin is 60%. You want 20% of revenue left as contribution after ads. That means ads can take 40% of revenue, so target MER = 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5.

If you want 30% left after ads, ads can take 30% of revenue and target MER is 3.3. Higher profit goals mean higher MER targets and usually less growth.

Using MER to manage budgets

  • MER above target: you probably have room to increase spend.
  • MER below target: look at which channels or campaigns to cut, and check offer and site conversion.
  • MER falling as spend rises: normal diminishing returns. Find the spend level where marginal MER still meets your goal.

Track MER weekly and monthly. Daily MER is noisy, especially around promotions.

What MER can't tell you

  • Which channel or campaign drove the result
  • Whether revenue came from ads or from email, SEO and word of mouth
  • Whether you're acquiring new customers or just reselling to existing ones (use new customer MER for that)

That's why I use MER alongside platform ROAS and POAS, not instead of them.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing MER across periods with very different promotions
  • Leaving out a platform's spend from the calculation
  • Setting an MER target without knowing contribution margin
  • Expecting MER to stay flat while spend doubles

Frequently asked questions

What is MER in e-commerce?

MER, or marketing efficiency ratio, is total revenue divided by total marketing or ad spend over the same period. It is also called blended ROAS because it does not rely on any single platform's attribution.

What is the difference between MER and ROAS?

ROAS uses revenue attributed by one ad platform to that platform's spend. MER uses all store revenue and all ad spend. ROAS helps optimise inside a platform while MER shows overall efficiency of the marketing budget.

What is a good MER?

A good MER is one that covers your costs and leaves the profit you need. Calculate it from contribution margin and your target marketing share of revenue. Many e-commerce brands aim for an MER somewhere around 3 to 5, but it varies by margin and growth stage.
