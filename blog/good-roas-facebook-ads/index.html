What Is a Good ROAS for Facebook Ads in E-commerce?

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 7 min read · Nov 2026

Clients often tell me they want "a 4x ROAS" on Facebook ads. Sometimes that's a good goal. Sometimes a 2x ROAS would make them more money. A good ROAS isn't a benchmark you copy from another brand. It's a number you calculate from your own business.

Quick summary

  • A good ROAS is one above your break-even ROAS with room for profit.
  • Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin.
  • Many e-commerce brands land somewhere around 2–4x on Meta, but margins decide what's good for you.
  • Judge prospecting and retargeting differently, and check blended MER.

What ROAS actually measures

ROAS (return on ad spend) is attributed revenue divided by ad spend. If you spend $1,000 and Meta reports $3,000 in purchase value, ROAS is 3.0.

Two things ROAS does not tell you:

  • Profit. $3,000 in revenue could be very profitable or loss-making depending on product cost, shipping and fees.
  • Incrementality. Meta's attribution counts sales it touched, not only sales it caused.

Step 1: Calculate your break-even ROAS

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin %. Contribution margin is what's left from each sale after cost of goods, shipping, payment fees, packaging and an allowance for returns.

Contribution marginBreak-even ROAS
70%1.43
60%1.67
50%2.00
40%2.50
30%3.33

A store with 30% margins needs over 3.3x just to break even on the first order. I cover this in detail in how to calculate break-even ROAS for Shopify.

Step 2: Set a target ROAS

Your target sits above break-even and depends on your goal:

  • Profit now: target well above break-even (for example break-even × 1.3–1.5).
  • Growth: accept near break-even on first order if customers come back and buy again.
  • Launch or new market: accept below break-even for a short, planned period to gather data.

If you know customer lifetime value, you can set first-order targets lower. If most customers only buy once, you need first-order profitability.

Typical ROAS by campaign type

Within one account, ROAS varies a lot by campaign type. Rough patterns I see:

  • Retargeting: highest attributed ROAS, but least incremental.
  • Advantage+ Shopping: often strong, partly because it includes existing customers.
  • Broad prospecting: lower ROAS, but this is where new customers come from.
  • Creative testing: lowest, because you're paying to learn.

Cutting prospecting because its ROAS is lower than retargeting usually shrinks the business over time.

Look beyond Meta's reported ROAS

Meta's ROAS depends on the attribution setting (for example 7-day click, 1-day view) and on tracking quality. To get a fuller picture:

  • Check blended MER (total revenue ÷ total ad spend). See MER explained.
  • Compare with Shopify revenue and new customer counts.
  • Consider POAS (profit on ad spend) when products have very different margins. See POAS vs ROAS.
  • Make sure the Conversions API is set up so purchases aren't under-reported.

What to do if ROAS is below target

Start with tracking, then offer and landing page, then creative, then audiences and structure. My guide on why Meta ads ROAS drops covers the nine most common causes.

Common mistakes

  • Using another brand's ROAS as your target
  • Judging every campaign by the same ROAS target
  • Ignoring returns and discounts in margin calculations
  • Comparing ROAS across different attribution settings

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROAS for Facebook ads?

A good ROAS is one that is above your break-even ROAS with enough room for profit. Many e-commerce brands aim for roughly 2 to 4 on Meta, but a store with high margins can be profitable at a lower ROAS and a low-margin store may need a much higher one.

Is a ROAS of 2 good?

It depends on your margins. With a 60 percent contribution margin, break-even ROAS is about 1.67, so a ROAS of 2 is profitable. With a 30 percent margin, break-even ROAS is about 3.3, so a ROAS of 2 loses money on the first order.

Why is my retargeting ROAS much higher than prospecting?

Retargeting reaches people who were already close to buying, so attributed ROAS is naturally high. Some of those sales would have happened anyway. Judge retargeting on incrementality and look at blended results across the whole account.
