10 Facebook Ad Hooks That Work for DTC Brands

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 7 min read · Nov 2026

Most people decide whether to keep watching an ad in the first second or two. If the opening doesn't stop the scroll, the rest of your video, offer and landing page never get a chance. That's why the hook is the cheapest thing to improve and the most valuable thing to test.

Quick summary

  • The hook is the first 1–3 seconds (or the image and headline of a static ad).
  • Use proven formulas, then adapt them to your customer's language.
  • Test hooks by changing only the opening and keeping the body the same.
  • Judge on hook rate first, then CTR and cost per purchase.

10 hook formulas with examples

1. Call out the person

"If you have curly hair and live somewhere humid, watch this." Instantly tells the right person this ad is for them.

2. Name the problem

"My lower back used to hurt every time I sat at my desk." Starts where the customer already is.

3. The unexpected result

"I didn't think a $30 pillow would fix my sleep." Creates curiosity with a believable claim.

4. Stop doing this

"Stop buying cheap phone cases that crack in a month." A clear warning works because it suggests the viewer might be making a mistake.

5. Show the result first

Open on the finished outcome (the clean kitchen, the styled outfit), then show how you got there.

6. The honest review

"I've used this for 30 days. Here's my honest opinion." Feels like content, not an ad.

7. Comparison

"I tried three popular brands. Only one was worth it." Great for crowded categories.

8. The number

"3 reasons this is the only bag I travel with." Numbers promise a quick, structured payoff.

9. Social proof

"Over 10,000 five-star reviews, and this is why." Only use numbers that are true and verifiable.

10. Pattern interrupt

An unusual visual: the product dropped, poured, stretched or tested in a surprising way. No words needed.

Write hooks in your customer's words

The best hooks rarely come from a brainstorm. Pull phrases from reviews, support emails, Reddit threads and comments on your ads. If customers say "finally fits my wide feet", that beats any slogan your team writes.

How to measure a hook

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Hook rate3-second views ÷ impressionsDoes the opening stop the scroll?
Hold rateThruPlays ÷ 3-second viewsDoes the rest keep attention?
CTR (link)Link clicks ÷ impressionsDoes it create intent?
Cost per purchaseSpend ÷ purchasesDoes it sell?

A high hook rate with a low hold rate means the opening works but the body doesn't. A low hook rate means test new openings before touching anything else.

How to test hooks cheaply

  1. Take one video that already gets some sales.
  2. Create 3–5 versions with different first 3 seconds, same body.
  3. Put them in one ad set in your testing campaign.
  4. After a few days, keep the hooks with the best hook rate and CTR, and let the winner run long enough to judge cost per purchase.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with the logo or brand name
  • Slow intros before the point
  • Hooks that promise something the product can't deliver
  • Changing hook, body and offer at the same time, so you learn nothing

Frequently asked questions

What is a hook in a Facebook ad?

The hook is the first one to three seconds of a video ad, or the main image and headline of a static ad. Its only job is to stop the scroll and make the right person keep watching.

What is a good hook rate on Facebook ads?

Hook rate is 3-second video views divided by impressions. Benchmarks vary by niche and placement, so compare hooks against your own account average and keep the ones clearly above it.

How do I test ad hooks?

Keep the body of the video the same and change only the first few seconds. Run the variations in the same ad set and compare hook rate, then CTR and cost per purchase once there is enough spend.
