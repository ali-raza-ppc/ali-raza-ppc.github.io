Do Facebook Lookalike Audiences Still Work? How to Use Them Today

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 8 min read · Nov 2026

A few years ago, lookalike audiences were the backbone of almost every Facebook ads account. Upload your customers, build a 1% lookalike, scale. Today most media buyers talk about broad targeting and Advantage+ instead, so a fair question is whether lookalikes are still worth using.

Short answer: they still work, but they're no longer the default. Here's how I use them in client accounts now.

Quick summary

  • Lookalikes find people similar to a seed audience you provide.
  • Meta's delivery has improved so much that broad often matches or beats them.
  • Use lookalikes as a signal, a test against broad, or for value-based seeds.
  • Seed quality matters far more than lookalike size.

What a lookalike audience is

A lookalike audience is built from a source (seed) audience, such as a customer list, purchasers from your pixel or people who engaged with your Instagram account. Meta finds people in a chosen country who share characteristics with that seed. You choose a size from 1% (closest match) up to 10% of the country's population.

Why lookalikes matter less than they used to

  • Delivery got smarter. Meta's systems, including the Andromeda retrieval model, already use purchase signals to find buyers inside broad audiences.
  • Audiences expand anyway. With Advantage+ audience, your targeting inputs act as suggestions, and Meta may go beyond them when it predicts better results.
  • Privacy changes reduced the data available to build precise seed profiles.
  • Creative now does more of the targeting. What your ad says and shows helps decide who sees it.

When lookalikes still make sense

SituationWhy a lookalike helps
New pixel with few purchasesA customer list gives Meta a head start it can't get from pixel data yet
High-value or repeat customersA value-based seed points delivery toward your best buyers, not just any buyer
Lead generationA seed of qualified or closed leads can improve lead quality versus broad
TestingRunning lookalike vs broad side by side tells you what works in your account
Advantage+ audience suggestionsA lookalike can be the suggestion that steers early delivery

Choosing the right seed

The seed is everything. A lookalike of "everyone who visited the website" will find people who browse. A lookalike of "customers who bought twice" will find people more like your real buyers.

  1. Customer list with value (lifetime value or total spend) for a value-based lookalike
  2. Repeat purchasers or top 25% customers by spend
  3. All purchasers from the last 180 days
  4. Initiate checkout or add to cart if purchases are too few
  5. Engagers or video viewers only for brand-new accounts with no customers

Meta needs at least 100 people from a single country in the seed, but larger, high-quality seeds (often 1,000+) give better results. Upload as many identifiers as you can (email, phone, name, city, country) so more of the list matches.

How to test lookalikes against broad

  1. Use the same campaign, budget approach, creatives and optimisation event.
  2. Create one ad set with a 1–3% purchaser lookalike and one with broad targeting.
  3. Run long enough to exit the learning phase and collect meaningful purchases.
  4. Compare cost per purchase and, if possible, new-customer rate and average order value.

Whichever wins, keep the structure simple. If broad wins, you can still add the lookalike as an audience suggestion. My broad vs interest targeting guide covers the same testing logic for interests.

Common mistakes

  • Building lookalikes from low-intent seeds like all page likes
  • Stacking many lookalike sizes in separate ad sets and splitting the budget
  • Excluding so many audiences that delivery has nowhere to go
  • Never refreshing seed lists as the customer base grows
  • Assuming a lookalike will fix weak creative or a poor offer

Frequently asked questions

Do Facebook lookalike audiences still work?

Yes, but their role has changed. With Advantage+ audience and broad targeting, Meta's delivery system often finds buyers without them. Lookalikes are now most useful as a signal or a test against broad, especially for newer accounts and value-based seeds.

What is the best seed audience for a lookalike?

Your best customers, not everyone. Purchasers, repeat buyers or a customer list with purchase value usually beat website visitors or page engagers, because the seed tells Meta what a valuable customer looks like.

What lookalike size should I use?

Start with 1% for the closest match, then test 1–3% or 1–5% as you scale. Larger sizes reach more people but are less similar to the seed. In many accounts the difference shrinks once Meta is allowed to expand the audience.
