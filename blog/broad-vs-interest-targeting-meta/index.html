Broad Targeting vs Interest Targeting on Meta in 2026

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 7 min read · Nov 2026

A few years ago, finding the right interest audience was the core skill of a Facebook ads buyer. In 2026, Meta's delivery system relies much more on conversion data and on what your ads say and show. For many e-commerce accounts, broad targeting now beats carefully stacked interests. But not always.

Quick summary

  • Broad = location, age and language only; Meta finds buyers from data and creative.
  • For most e-commerce brands with good tracking, broad performs as well or better.
  • Interests still help for niche products, B2B and accounts with little data.
  • Test fairly: same creative, same budget, at least a week, judge on CPA.

What each option means

Broad targetingInterest targeting
InputsLocation, age, languageInterests, behaviours, demographics
Audience sizeVery largeSmaller
Who decides deliveryMeta's models, guided by data and creativeYour selections, then Meta within them
Best forE-commerce with conversion historyNiche offers, B2B, new accounts

Note that with Advantage+ audience, Meta can go beyond your interest selections anyway, treating them as suggestions rather than hard limits.

Why broad usually wins now

  • Better signals: with the Pixel and Conversions API, Meta knows far more about who buys than interest categories do.
  • Creative does the targeting: since the Andromeda update, Meta reads your ad content to decide who it fits.
  • More room to learn: large audiences help ad sets exit the learning phase faster.
  • Interest data is less precise: privacy changes have reduced the quality of some interest signals.

When interests still make sense

  • New accounts or pixels with few conversions to learn from.
  • Niche or B2B products where the buyer pool is small and specific.
  • Very low budgets where you can't afford the exploration broad needs.
  • Testing new markets to get early signal faster.

What about lookalikes?

Lookalikes based on purchasers or high-value customers can still help, especially for newer accounts. In many mature accounts, they now perform similarly to broad. Test them as one option, not a requirement.

How to test broad vs interests fairly

  1. Use the same creative in both.
  2. Give each the same budget, or use an A/B test in Ads Manager.
  3. Run for at least 7 days and enough spend for several purchases each.
  4. Compare cost per purchase and ROAS, not CTR or CPM.
  5. Check overlap; similar audiences can compete with each other.

The structure I usually start with

For established Shopify stores: one broad or Advantage+ prospecting campaign with diverse creative, a small creative testing campaign and light retargeting. For newer stores: one broad ad set plus one interest-based ad set, then shift budget to whichever wins. Details in my account structure guide.

Common mistakes

  • Stacking dozens of interests into one ad set
  • Splitting budget across many small interest ad sets
  • Going broad with weak tracking and expecting miracles
  • Judging broad after two days

Frequently asked questions

Does interest targeting still work on Facebook ads?

Interest targeting still works in some cases, such as niche B2B products, new accounts with little data or very specific audiences. For most e-commerce brands with good tracking, broad targeting with strong creative performs as well or better.

What is broad targeting on Meta?

Broad targeting means setting only basic limits such as location, age and language, and letting Meta's delivery system decide who sees the ad based on conversion data and the ad's content.

How do I test broad vs interest targeting?

Run the same creative and budget in two ad sets or campaigns, one broad and one with interests, for at least a week, and compare cost per purchase and ROAS rather than CTR.
