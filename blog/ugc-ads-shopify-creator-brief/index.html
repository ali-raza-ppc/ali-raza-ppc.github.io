UGC Ads for Shopify: How to Brief Creators That Convert

Ali RazaPerformance Marketing Specialist · 8 min read · Nov 2026

UGC (user-generated content) ads are short videos of real people using and talking about your product. On Meta and TikTok they often outperform polished studio ads because they look like the content people already scroll through. But most UGC that brands receive is unusable for ads, and the reason is almost always the brief.

Here's how I brief creators so the videos come back ready to test.

Quick summary

  • One brief = one message. Don't ask a creator to cover everything.
  • Give 3–5 hook options and ask for several takes of the opening.
  • List must-show moments, objections to handle and things to avoid.
  • Agree paid usage rights and raw footage in writing before filming.

Why most UGC fails as an ad

  • The first three seconds are a slow intro ("Hey guys, so today…").
  • The video tries to mention every feature and lands none.
  • The product appears late or isn't shown clearly.
  • No clear reason to buy now.

All four are brief problems, not creator problems.

The UGC brief template

SectionWhat to write
Product & audienceWhat it is, who it's for, the situation they're in
Main messageOne sentence: the single reason to buy
Hooks3–5 opening lines or visuals to choose from
Key points2–3 benefits, in the customer's words (use reviews)
ObjectionThe main doubt to answer (price, size, does it work?)
Must-showProduct in hand within 3 seconds, the result, packaging
AvoidClaims you can't make, competitor names, music with rights issues
Format9:16, 20–45 seconds, natural light, captions-friendly speech
DeliverablesNumber of videos, hook variations, raw clips
Rights & deadlinePaid usage duration, platforms, whitelisting, delivery date

Pick an angle per brief

Different angles reach different people, which matters more since Meta's Andromeda update. Brief one angle per video:

  • Problem–solution: "I was tired of X, then I found this."
  • Unboxing / first impression: genuine reaction to the product.
  • Before and after: where it's honest and allowed.
  • Comparison: "I tried three of these, here's why I kept this one."
  • Gift angle: who it's perfect for and why.
  • Routine / day-in-the-life: the product in a real moment of use.

Ask for hook variations

The hook decides whether anyone watches the rest. Ask each creator for the same body with 3 different opening lines. That turns one video into three testable ads for very little extra cost. My post on Facebook ad hooks has examples you can put straight into briefs.

Usage rights and whitelisting

  • Paid usage: how long you can run the video as an ad and on which platforms.
  • Raw footage: lets you re-edit hooks, captions and lengths later.
  • Whitelisting / partnership ads: running ads from the creator's handle, which often adds social proof.

Get these in writing before filming. Re-negotiating after a video becomes a winner is expensive.

How many videos to order

For a first round, I usually suggest 4–6 creators, one angle each, with 3 hooks per video. That gives 12–18 ads to test, enough to find a winning angle without blowing the budget. Test them using a simple creative testing framework.

Editing for the feed

  • Cut every second before the hook.
  • Add captions; many people watch without sound.
  • Show the product and price or offer clearly near the end.
  • Export 9:16 for Reels, Stories and TikTok, and 4:5 for Feed.

Common mistakes

  • Writing a script word for word, so it sounds like an ad
  • Briefing every creator with the same angle
  • Forgetting usage rights until the ad is already working
  • Judging UGC after one day of spend

Frequently asked questions

What should a UGC creator brief include?

A good UGC brief includes the product and who it is for, the one main message, three to five hook options, the key benefits and objections to address, must-show moments, things to avoid, format and length, deliverables, deadline and usage rights.

How much should I pay UGC creators?

Rates vary widely by creator, market and usage rights. Many brands pay per video plus extra for paid usage rights or raw footage. Agree usage rights and duration in writing before filming.

Do UGC ads work better than studio ads?

UGC often performs well on Meta and TikTok because it looks native to the feed and feels like a recommendation. Results still depend on the hook, message and offer, so test UGC against your other formats rather than assuming.
